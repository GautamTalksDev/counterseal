Privacy

Subprocessors

When a business uses Counterseal, we process its data on its behalf. One company helps us do that: Cloudflare.

Last updated 10 October 2026 (no new subprocessor; the list below now names each Cloudflare product, and single sign-on, alert channels and AI model APIs are added to who is not a subprocessor)

Cloudflare's Customer Data Processing Addendum is part of its subscription agreement. It names Cloudflare as a processor (or subprocessor) and covers transfers out of the EU, the UK and Switzerland with the EU Standard Contractual Clauses, the UK Addendum, and Cloudflare's EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework certification (as that page said on 9 October 2026).

Workers Logs are off for this service, so Cloudflare does not keep our request logs or our log lines. Cloudflare may still process request metadata, such as IP addresses, to run and protect its network, under its own terms.

Who is not a subprocessor, and why

  • Your browser's push service (Apple, Google, Mozilla or Microsoft, depending on your browser), only if you turn on alerts. Your browser picks it, not us. We send it an empty wake-up for your device and nothing else: no text, no request, no name. Your device then asks Counterseal for the details over your own session.
  • The APIs you connect (GitHub, Cloudflare, Railway, Stripe, Supabase, Vercel, Neon, Fly.io, Resend, Shopify, and for the Budget seal the AI model APIs of OpenAI and Anthropic). Your agent's requests go to them because you told us to send them there, with your own key; they are your providers, under your agreements with them.
  • Your identity provider, only if an owner turns on single sign-on. Your team chooses it and has its own agreement with it. Counterseal sends your people's browsers to it and reads back a signed ID token; it receives nothing else from us.
  • Your team's alert channels (Slack, Discord or Google Chat), only if an owner adds one. You choose them; they get a short alert text with a label, a rule id and a link, never a request body or key.
  • GitHub, for deploy gates: Counterseal fetches GitHub's public signing keys to check a workflow's OIDC token. Nothing about you is sent to GitHub by us.
  • Nobody else. No analytics, no advertising, no error tracking, no email sender, no payment processor (there is no billing yet), no fonts or scripts from other sites. The npm registry and GitHub host the open source command line tool and its source code; they never receive customer data from the service.

Changes

Before we add a subprocessor, we update this page first and give the date. Businesses with a data processing agreement with us get the notice that agreement sets (the template says 30 days), and can object. To hear about changes, watch this page's history in the public repository (site/public/privacy/subprocessors), or ask to be on the notice list. Questions: developwith.gt@gmail.com.
